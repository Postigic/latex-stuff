\begin{claim}
    If $AB$ is invertible and either $A$ or $B$ is invertible, then the other factor is also invertible.
\end{claim}

\begin{proof}
    Assume WLOG that $A$ is invertible, so $A$ is $n \times n$ for some $n$. Since $AB$ is defined, $B$ must 
    have $n$ rows, say $B$ is $n \times m$, so $AB$ is $n \times m$ as well. But $AB$ is invertible, hence 
    square, so $m = n$. Thus $B$ is also $n \times n$.

    \medskip

    It remains to show $B$ is invertible. Since $A$ is invertible, so is $A^{-1}$, and
    \[
        A^{-1}(AB) = (A^{-1}A)B = IB = B.
    \]
    Thus $B$ is a product of two invertible matrices, $A^{-1}$ and $AB$, and is therefore itself invertible.

    \medskip

    Hence the other factor is also invertible.
\end{proof}
